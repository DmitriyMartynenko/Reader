Cyprus. A Street

#1096 [D]
Enter Iago and Roderigo.

#1097 IAGO
Here, stand behind this bulk. Straight will he come.
Wear thy good rapier bare, and put it home.
Quick, quick, fear nothing; I’ll be at thy elbow.
It makes us, or it mars us, think on that,
And fix most firm thy resolution.

#1098 RODERIGO
Be near at hand, I may miscarry in ’t.

#1099 IAGO
Here, at thy hand. Be bold, and take thy stand.

#1100 [D]
[_Retires to a little distance._]

#1101 RODERIGO
I have no great devotion to the deed;
And yet he hath given me satisfying reasons.
’Tis but a man gone. Forth, my sword; he dies.

#1102 [D]
[_Goes to his stand._]

#1103 IAGO
I have rubb’d this young quat almost to the sense,
And he grows angry. Now, whether he kill Cassio,
Or Cassio him, or each do kill the other,
Every way makes my gain. Live Roderigo,
He calls me to a restitution large
Of gold and jewels that I bobb’d from him,
As gifts to Desdemona.
It must not be. If Cassio do remain,
He hath a daily beauty in his life
That makes me ugly. And besides, the Moor
May unfold me to him; there stand I in much peril.
No, he must die. But so, I hear him coming.

#1104 [D]
Enter Cassio.

#1105 RODERIGO
I know his gait; ’tis he. Villain, thou diest!

#1106 [D]
[_Rushes out, and makes a pass at Cassio._]

#1107 CASSIO
That thrust had been mine enemy indeed,
But that my coat is better than thou know’st.
I will make proof of thine.

#1108 [D]
[_Draws, and wounds Roderigo._]

#1109 RODERIGO
O, I am slain!

#1110 [D]
[_Iago rushes from his post, cuts Cassio behind in the leg, and exit._]

#1111 CASSIO
I am maim’d forever. Help, ho! murder, murder!

#1112 [D]
[_Falls._]

#1113 [D]
Enter Othello.

#1114 OTHELLO
The voice of Cassio. Iago keeps his word.

#1115 RODERIGO
O, villain that I am!

#1116 OTHELLO
It is even so.

#1117 CASSIO
O, help, ho! light! a surgeon!

#1118 OTHELLO
’Tis he. O brave Iago, honest and just,
That hast such noble sense of thy friend’s wrong!
Thou teachest me,—minion, your dear lies dead,
And your unbless’d fate hies. Strumpet, I come!
Forth of my heart those charms, thine eyes, are blotted;
Thy bed, lust-stain’d, shall with lust’s blood be spotted.

#1119 [D]
[_Exit._]

#1120 [D]
Enter Lodovico and Gratiano.

#1121 CASSIO
What, ho! No watch? No passage? murder, murder!

#1122 GRATIANO
’Tis some mischance; the cry is very direful.

#1123 CASSIO
O, help!

#1124 LODOVICO
Hark!

#1125 RODERIGO
O wretched villain!

#1126 LODOVICO
Two or three groan. It is a heavy night.
These may be counterfeits. Let’s think’t unsafe
To come in to the cry without more help.

#1127 RODERIGO
Nobody come? Then shall I bleed to death.

#1128 [D]
Enter Iago with a light.

#1129 LODOVICO
Hark!

#1130 GRATIANO
Here’s one comes in his shirt, with light and weapons.

#1131 IAGO
Who’s there? Whose noise is this that cries on murder?

#1132 LODOVICO
We do not know.

#1133 IAGO
Did not you hear a cry?

#1134 CASSIO
Here, here! for heaven’s sake, help me!

#1135 IAGO
What’s the matter?

#1136 GRATIANO
This is Othello’s ancient, as I take it.

#1137 LODOVICO
The same indeed, a very valiant fellow.

#1138 IAGO
What are you here that cry so grievously?

#1139 CASSIO
Iago? O, I am spoil’d, undone by villains!
Give me some help.

#1140 IAGO
O me, lieutenant! What villains have done this?

#1141 CASSIO
I think that one of them is hereabout,
And cannot make away.

#1142 IAGO
O treacherous villains!
[_To Lodovico and Gratiano._] What are you there?
Come in and give some help.

#1143 RODERIGO
O, help me here!

#1144 CASSIO
That’s one of them.

#1145 IAGO
O murderous slave! O villain!

#1146 [D]
[_Stabs Roderigo._]

#1147 RODERIGO
O damn’d Iago! O inhuman dog!

#1148 IAGO
Kill men i’ the dark! Where be these bloody thieves?
How silent is this town! Ho! murder! murder!
What may you be? Are you of good or evil?

#1149 LODOVICO
As you shall prove us, praise us.

#1150 IAGO
Signior Lodovico?

#1151 LODOVICO
He, sir.

#1152 IAGO
I cry you mercy. Here’s Cassio hurt by villains.

#1153 GRATIANO
Cassio!

#1154 IAGO
How is’t, brother?

#1155 CASSIO
My leg is cut in two.

#1156 IAGO
Marry, heaven forbid!
Light, gentlemen, I’ll bind it with my shirt.

#1157 [D]
Enter Bianca.

#1158 BIANCA
What is the matter, ho? Who is’t that cried?

#1159 IAGO
Who is’t that cried?

#1160 BIANCA
O my dear Cassio, my sweet Cassio! O Cassio, Cassio, Cassio!

#1161 IAGO
O notable strumpet! Cassio, may you suspect
Who they should be that have thus mangled you?

#1162 CASSIO
No.

#1163 GRATIANO
I am sorry to find you thus; I have been to seek you.

#1164 IAGO
Lend me a garter. So.—O, for a chair,
To bear him easily hence!

#1165 BIANCA
Alas, he faints! O Cassio, Cassio, Cassio!

#1166 IAGO
Gentlemen all, I do suspect this trash
To be a party in this injury.
Patience awhile, good Cassio. Come, come;
Lend me a light. Know we this face or no?
Alas, my friend and my dear countryman
Roderigo? No. Yes, sure; O heaven! Roderigo.

#1167 GRATIANO
What, of Venice?

#1168 IAGO
Even he, sir. Did you know him?

#1169 GRATIANO
Know him? Ay.

#1170 IAGO
Signior Gratiano? I cry you gentle pardon.
These bloody accidents must excuse my manners,
That so neglected you.

#1171 GRATIANO
I am glad to see you.

#1172 IAGO
How do you, Cassio? O, a chair, a chair!

#1173 GRATIANO
Roderigo!

#1174 IAGO
He, he, ’tis he.

#1175 [D]
[_A chair brought in._]

#1176 IAGO
O, that’s well said; the chair.
Some good man bear him carefully from hence,
I’ll fetch the general’s surgeon. [_To Bianca_] For you, mistress,
Save you your labour. He that lies slain here, Cassio,
Was my dear friend. What malice was between you?

#1177 CASSIO
None in the world. Nor do I know the man.

#1178 IAGO
[_To Bianca._] What, look you pale?—O, bear him out o’ the air.

#1179 [D]
[_Cassio and Roderigo are borne off._]

#1180 IAGO
Stay you, good gentlemen.—Look you pale, mistress?
Do you perceive the gastness of her eye?
Nay, if you stare, we shall hear more anon.
Behold her well. I pray you, look upon her.
Do you see, gentlemen? Nay, guiltiness will speak
Though tongues were out of use.

#1181 [D]
Enter Emilia.

#1182 EMILIA
’Las, what’s the matter? What’s the matter, husband?

#1183 IAGO
Cassio hath here been set on in the dark
By Roderigo, and fellows that are ’scap’d.
He’s almost slain, and Roderigo dead.

#1184 EMILIA
Alas, good gentleman! alas, good Cassio!

#1185 IAGO
This is the fruit of whoring. Prithee, Emilia,
Go know of Cassio where he supp’d tonight.
What, do you shake at that?

#1186 BIANCA
He supp’d at my house, but I therefore shake not.

#1187 IAGO
O, did he so? I charge you go with me.

#1188 EMILIA
Fie, fie upon thee, strumpet!

#1189 BIANCA
I am no strumpet, but of life as honest
As you that thus abuse me.

#1190 EMILIA
As I? Foh! fie upon thee!

#1191 IAGO
Kind gentlemen, let’s go see poor Cassio dress’d.
Come, mistress, you must tell’s another tale.
Emilia, run you to the citadel,
And tell my lord and lady what hath happ’d.
Will you go on afore? [_Aside._] This is the night
That either makes me or fordoes me quite.

#1192 [D]
[_Exeunt._]
